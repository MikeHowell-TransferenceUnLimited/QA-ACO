CGA graphics, introduced by IBM in the early 1980s, marked one of the first steps toward bringing color visuals to personal computers. Although limited to a small palette of bold and sometimes unusual colors, it provided a foundation for early PC gaming and digital art. Despite its simplicity by modern standards, CGA remains an iconic milestone in the evolution of computer graphics.

The Hercules graphics standard was known for its crisp monochrome display, offering sharp text and higher resolution graphics than competing options of its time. Originally designed for business applications, it became popular among early PC users who valued clarity over color. Even without vibrant visuals, Hercules played a significant role in shaping the early personal computing landscape.
