Duesenberg began in the early 1900s with brothers Fred and August Duesenberg, talented self-taught engineers who first made their mark building racing engines and high-performance automobiles. Their early company, the Duesenberg Motor Company, supplied engines for race cars, boats, and even aircraft engines during World War I. By the 1920s, they were known for engineering excellence, particularly in American open-wheel racing. This racing background gave the Duesenberg name a reputation for speed, innovation, and resilience. Their victories in events like the Indianapolis 500 helped establish the brand as a symbol of elite automotive engineering.

The turning point came in 1926 when E.L. Cord purchased Duesenberg and set out to build the finest American luxury car ever produced. Under Cord's direction, the brothers designed what became the Duesenberg Model J, unveiled in 1928 and immediately recognized as one of the most advanced and luxurious cars in the world. It featured cutting-edge performance, with a straight-eight engine capable of speeds far beyond its competitors. Wealthy buyers could commission custom bodies from top coachbuilders, making each Model J unique. The car quickly became a status symbol for the wealthy, Hollywood stars, and royalty.

Despite its excellence, the Great Depression severely reduced the market for ultra-luxury vehicles, and Duesenberg struggled financially during the 1930s. The company attempted to stay relevant with even more advanced versions of the Model J, including the supercharged SJ and JN variants, which pushed the boundaries of performance at the time. However, as Cord’s empire weakened, Duesenberg production slowed and eventually ceased in 1937. Even so, the brand’s legacy of engineering brilliance endured, and surviving cars became some of the most valuable collector vehicles in the world. Today, a Duesenberg remains a symbol of American craftsmanship, innovation, and prewar luxury engineering at its peak.
